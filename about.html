Who We Are

Who We Are

Relief Projects India (RPI) is a grassroots nonprofit organisation working with children and families in the rural villages of Usilampatti Taluk, Madurai District, Tamil Nadu. Founded in 2009, RPI began with a simple belief: every child deserves access to quality education, regardless of poverty or social circumstance.

What started as a small network of free evening tuition centres for children attending government schools has grown into a community development organisation working across rural Tamil Nadu. Today, RPI works to improve educational opportunities, promote health and wellbeing, empower women and adolescent girls, and strengthen child protection through long-term partnerships with local communities.

At the heart of our work is Mountain View School, an English-medium primary school established in 2015 to provide children from rural farming families with the education and confidence to build brighter futures. Beyond the school, our community programmes address the wider challenges that affect children's lives—including health awareness, life-skills education, women's empowerment, and support for girls at risk of leaving school or marrying early.

Relief Projects India has a long-term commitment to the communities we serve. For more than fifteen years, we have worked alongside families, Panchayat leaders, schools, and government agencies, building relationships based on trust and partnership. We believe lasting change happens when communities are supported consistently over time—helping children, especially girls, to stay in school, realise their potential, and create better futures for themselves and their families.

Our Story

How RPI Has Grown

From a small evening tuition programme in 2009 to a school serving over 300 students and community programmes reaching thousands of families, RPI has grown steadily by listening to what communities need most.

2009

RPI Founded

Relief Projects India is established in Usilampatti, beginning with free evening tuition centres for children attending government schools, with a particular emphasis on supporting girls' education and reducing school dropout rates.

2010–14

Health & Protection Programmes

RPI expanded into health and protection work — maternal and child health support, awareness programmes for parents on girls' education and preventing early marriage, and, in collaboration with government health facilities and local NGOs, haemoglobin testing, TB and HIV screening, and free eye camps. This period also marked the start of our collaboration with ICDS, the District Child Protection Unit, RNTCP, and ICTC.

2015

Mountain View School Opens

Mountain View School's story begins with a small nursery for fewer than 30 children, aged 3 to 5, drawn from economically disadvantaged villages nearby.

2016–25

Steady Growth

What started as a modest response to a real need soon grows: the school earns official recognition as a nursery and primary school, and enrolment rises from just 54 students in 2016 to over 300 students today.
RPI expands its community reach across 25 villages, adding adolescent development, women's empowerment, life-skills, career guidance, and cervical cancer screening programmes.

Today

305 Students & Growing

Mountain View School serves 305 students from LKG to Grade 5. Ten free evening tuition centres run across villages. Community programmes now reach more than 14,000 people.

Future

Expanding Opportunities

Our vision is to expand Mountain View School through Grade 12, strengthen our community programmes, and reach more children and families across rural Tamil Nadu.

How We Make a Difference

Two Long-Term Initiatives

To create lasting social change by improving the education, health, protection, and well-being of marginalised children and women in rural communities.

Mountain View School

Mountain View School is working toward a phased expansion of our academic programme: extending first from 5th to 8th standard (Middle School), and ultimately through to 12th standard. This growth will allow the students we've supported since kindergarten to complete their full education in a top-tier English-medium school.

Governance

Governance & Accountability

Monitoring & Evaluation

At Relief Projects India, we are committed to ensuring our programmes create real, measurable impact for the communities we serve. We regularly monitor and evaluate our work to ensure resources are used effectively and that our programmes continue to meet the actual needs of the community.

  • Our General Manager and social worker oversee our social programmes and evaluate their impact on an ongoing basis.
  • At Mountain View School, we track key outcomes including student enrolment, attendance, learning progress, and programme participation.
  • Our curriculum provider, Chrysalis, together with Board Member and professional educator Shiva Raj and school co-founder Susanna Turcotte, provide continuous professional development for teachers and monitor student progress.
  • We gather regular feedback from students, families, and community members to continually improve our programmes.
  • Progress is reviewed periodically by the Board of Trustees to ensure our work remains aligned with our mission and goals.

This ongoing process of reflection and improvement keeps us accountable both to the communities we serve and to the donors who make our work possible.

Financial Accountability

Relief Projects India is a registered public charitable trust, established under the Indian Trusts Act and operating in Usilampatti, Madurai District, Tamil Nadu. We are committed to full transparency in how donations are received and used.

  • All funds received are recorded and utilised in accordance with our trust deed and applicable Indian regulations.
  • Donations directly support our educational and community programmes, including Mountain View School and our wider humanitarian initiatives in rural Tamil Nadu.
  • Our accounts are maintained and reviewed to ensure funds are used responsibly and for their intended purpose.
  • Accounts are audited annually by KCS Associates, Madurai.
  • Financial statements and records are available to donors upon request.

Collaboration

Partners

Relief Projects India (RPI) works in close partnership with government departments — including ICDS, DCPU, One Stop Centre, the Social Welfare Department, ICTC, NTEP/RNTCP, Government Health Facilities, All Women Police Stations, RTO, and the Fire Department — to provide awareness, counselling, referrals, and access to welfare services for children, women, and vulnerable communities.

The organisation also collaborates with hospitals, eye hospitals, schools, colleges, NGOs, and community-based organisations to conduct medical camps, eye camps, haemoglobin testing, cervical cancer screening, and cataract referral services.

RPI further works closely with Panchayat leaders, government officials, village volunteers, and local stakeholders to strengthen community participation and improve protection, health, and educational outcomes.

Looking Ahead

The Next Five Years

Over the next five years, Relief Projects India will expand Mountain View School from Grade 5 to Grade 10, enabling more rural children to access quality education closer to home. RPI will also grow its network of Evening Tuition Centres and increase student enrolment, ensuring more children benefit from academic support, life skills education, mentoring, and child protection services.

RPI will strengthen its efforts to reduce school dropout, child marriage, and child labour, while supporting children to pursue higher education and meaningful careers. Through stronger linkages with government welfare schemes, healthcare services, and livelihood opportunities, RPI aims to improve the quality of life of children, women, and vulnerable families — building empowered, resilient communities.
